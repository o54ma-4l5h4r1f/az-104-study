# D1 — how it all connects

Four pictures, not forty facts. Each one links things that only make sense *together*. Dashed, muted boxes are **forward-links** — constructs from another domain (mostly D4 networking, which you already own) drawn so you can see the family resemblance without the detail leaking in here.

> **RBAC in depth, two ways:** a [complete reference view](../visuals/rbac-complete.html) (every roles/permissions concept + comparison tables on one printable page) and an [interactive walkthrough](../interactive/rbac-roles.html) (one scenario — Alex reads a blob — including the look-alikes: Entra roles, deny assignments, classic admins, PIM).

## 1 · The scope spine — what inherits down

The backbone of everything in D1.2 and D1.3. Five scope levels stack from the tenant root down to a single resource. **RBAC, Azure Policy and resource locks all flow *down* this spine** — set one high and it applies to everything below, and a child ==!cannot opt out==. The one exception is tags: they do **not** inherit.

## 2 · Identity → access bridge

D1.1 (who exists) and D1.2 (what they can touch) meet at one object: the **role assignment**. It binds a *principal* (a user, group, service principal or managed identity) to a *role definition* at a *scope* on the spine above. Licenses are the odd one out — an entitlement attached to the identity, ==!not a role assignment==. And the whole left side is a **different plane** from the right: Entra is not Azure RBAC.

## 3 · The four gates — why an operation was denied

The single highest-value picture in the domain, in recap form (the full version lives on the Azure Policy page: [D1.3.1](../objective/D1.3.1.html)). One request is checked against **four independent gates**. Any one of them denies on its own — it is ==!not first-match== and ==!not a chain==. The classic trap: an Owner is blocked, and the answer is Policy or a lock, not RBAC. The muted box is the cousin you already know from firewalls.
