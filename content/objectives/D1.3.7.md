## In one sentence
A management group is a container that sits *above* subscriptions, so one Azure Policy or one RBAC role assignment can be applied once and inherited by every subscription, resource group, and resource under it.

## Why it exists
The resource hierarchy stops at the subscription. Resources go into resource groups, resource groups go into subscriptions — fine for one or two subscriptions. With twenty subscriptions across teams, you would otherwise script the same policy and the same role assignment onto each one, and miss some. A management group adds a tier above the subscription so you assign governance once at the top and it flows down. It is a ==central firewall policy pushed from a manager to many edge devices==: write the rule at the parent, every child enforces it, and the local owner cannot turn it off.

## Mental model
One tenant, one root, a tree of management groups, subscriptions as leaves. Policy and RBAC inherit downward only.

## What actually matters
- **It is a scope above subscriptions, and inheritance only goes down.** A policy or role assigned on a management group applies to every nested management group, subscription, resource group, and resource beneath it. The subscription or resource owner ==!cannot override or opt out== of a policy set higher up. That is what makes it governance, not a suggestion.
- **One root, built in, named "Tenant root group".** Every Entra tenant has exactly one root management group. Its ID equals the ==@Microsoft Entra tenant ID==. It cannot be moved or deleted. New subscriptions land under it by default. Anything assigned on the root hits the entire directory — treat it as "must-have only".
- **The limits are testable numbers:**

| Limit | Exact value |
|---|---|
| Management groups per directory | ==@10,000== |
| Depth of management-group levels | up to ==@6 levels== (the root level and the subscription level do **not** count) |
| Parents per management group / per subscription | exactly ==@one parent== (many children allowed) |
| Where a brand-new management group is created | under the ==@root== by default |

- **All subscriptions in the tree must trust the same Entra tenant.** You cannot mix subscriptions from different tenants under one management group hierarchy.
- **Moving a subscription re-parents its inheritance.** A subscription has one parent management group; move it and it inherits the new parent's policies and RBAC and drops the old parent's. Only management groups and subscriptions can be children of a management group — ==!not resource groups directly==.
- **No default access to the root.** Nobody — not even a Global Administrator — has access to manage the root until a Global Administrator elevates to User Access Administrator across the tenant, then assigns roles. Changes can take up to ==@30 minutes== to appear because ARM caches the hierarchy.

**The three scopes, side by side** — a central firewall policy pushed down to the edges. RBAC and Azure Policy flow *down* from every tier to everything beneath it:

| Scope | Contains | Flows DOWN to its children | Its role as a scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==@Management group== | Other management groups and subscriptions | RBAC assignments, Azure Policy | Highest inheritable scope — set governance once at the top |
| ==@Subscription== | Resource groups | RBAC assignments, Azure Policy | ==@Billing== + ==@access-control== boundary; has exactly one parent MG |
| ==@Resource group== | Resources | RBAC assignments, Azure Policy | Lifecycle/delete boundary; ==!cannot be a child of a management group== |

## Where it lives
- **Portal (view/create):** `Azure portal > All services > Management + governance > Management Groups`, then **+ Add management group**. Fastest route later: search **Management groups** in **All services**. The navigation behaves like a file explorer — select a group name to drill down, select **(details)** to manage it.
- **Portal (add/move a subscription):** open the target management group > **Add subscription** > pick the subscription. To move one out, use the **…** at the end of the subscription's row > **Move**.
- **Azure CLI:** `az account management-group create --name 'Contoso'`, `... list`, `... show --name 'Contoso'`, `... update --name 'Contoso' --parent ContosoIT`, `... delete --name 'Contoso'`. Add a subscription: `az account management-group subscription add --name 'Contoso' --subscription {subId}`; remove with `... subscription remove`.
- **Azure PowerShell** (module `Az.Resources`): `New-AzManagementGroup -GroupId 'Contoso'`, `Get-AzManagementGroup`, `Update-AzManagementGroup -GroupId 'Contoso' -ParentId $parent.id`, `Remove-AzManagementGroup -GroupId 'Contoso'`. Add a subscription: `New-AzManagementGroupSubscription -GroupId 'Contoso' -SubscriptionId {subId}` (remove with `Remove-AzManagementGroupSubscription`). Note `-GroupId` is an alias of `-GroupName`.
- **Referencing a group as a scope** (for a role or policy assignment): `/providers/Microsoft.Management/managementGroups/{groupId}` — use the ==@ID, not the display name==.

## Exam traps
- **Inheritance direction.** Policy and RBAC flow ==down== from a management group to its subscriptions and resources — never up. A sub-level owner cannot override a policy inherited from above. A distractor will claim ==!the subscription owner can opt out==.
- **Depth of 6, and what counts.** The tree supports ==@6 management-group levels==, and the root level and the subscription level are ==!not counted==. Expect a question that tries to make you count the root or the subs into the 6.
- **One parent.** A subscription (and a management group) has exactly ==@one parent== management group. ==!"A subscription can belong to two management groups"== is false.
- **Same tenant only.** All subscriptions under one hierarchy must trust the ==@same Microsoft Entra tenant==. You cannot pull a subscription from another tenant into your management groups without moving its tenant first.
- **Root can't be deleted or moved; nobody has default access.** A Global Administrator must first ==+elevate access== (becoming User Access Administrator at tenant scope) before anyone can manage the root. ==!"The Global Admin already owns the root"== is a trap.
- **New subscriptions default to the root**, not to wherever you last worked. If a scenario says a new subscription isn't getting a policy, check whether it's still sitting ==under the root== rather than the intended management group.
- **Management group vs subscription vs resource group.** Only management groups give you an inheritable scope *above* subscriptions. You ==!cannot put a resource group directly under a management group==, and you ==!cannot apply a resource lock to a management group== (locks stop at subscription scope).
- **The 30-minute cache.** After a move, the portal may not reflect it for up to ==@30 minutes==. A scenario describing a "move that didn't work" is often just the cache — refresh the token.
