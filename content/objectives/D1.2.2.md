## In one sentence

A role assignment binds a security principal to a role definition at one scope — management group, subscription, resource group, or resource — and every child scope below it inherits that access.

> **Whole-picture reference:** the [RBAC complete view](../visuals/rbac-complete.html) puts scopes, roles, limits, deny assignments and ABAC on one page.

## Why it exists

You already know RBAC, least privilege and SSO. None of that is new. The Azure-specific part is **scope**: the exact same role means "everything in the tenant" or "one blob container" depending on where you pin it.

Think of it like a firewall rule. The rule logic is identical whether you apply it at the subnet or at a single NIC — placement decides the blast radius. Azure role scope works the same way. Owner at a subscription is dangerous. Owner at one resource group is routine. The role did not change; the scope did. Scope is how you give the dev team full control of their own resource group without handing them the rest of the subscription.

## Mental model

Four nested levels. Assign a role at any level and it flows **downward** to everything inside. It never flows up or sideways.

## What actually matters

The four scopes are one hierarchy. Pick the level, and the assignment covers that object plus everything beneath it. This is the firewall-rule-on-a-parent-object pattern you already know — apply at the top and it cascades; there is no "cascade up."

| Scope | An assignment here covers | Inherits |
|---|---|---|
| ==@Management group== | all subscriptions under it, nested management groups, and every resource in them | flows **down** to all children |
| ==@Subscription== | every resource group and resource in that subscription | flows **down** |
| ==@Resource group== | every resource in that resource group | flows **down** |
| ==@Resource== | that one object only — one VM, one storage account, one key vault | nothing below it to inherit |

Inheritance is ==down only== and ==additive==: assignments from every level above stack as a **union**, never a subtraction. Two assignments on the same principal grant the sum of both. To grant *less*, you assign at a narrower level in the first place — you cannot claw permissions back lower down.

- **Every assignment is three things: who + what + where.** Security principal (user, group, service principal, or managed identity) + role definition + scope. Drop any one and there is no assignment. "Who" can be a group — assign to the group, not person-by-person.
- **You cannot carve out an exception at a child** with another role assignment — Azure RBAC is an allow model, so a second assignment only ever *adds* permissions. To truly block, you need a deny assignment (Azure-managed, not something you create by hand) which is out of scope here.
- **`NotActions` is not a deny.** It subtracts operations from one role's own wildcard, but another assignment can still grant that operation. Do not treat it as a block.
- **Scope on the command line is a resource ID string.** Resource group: `/subscriptions/{subId}/resourceGroups/{name}`. Resource: that plus `/providers/{provider}/{type}/{resourceName}`. Management group: `/providers/Microsoft.Management/managementGroups/{name}`. The portal calls this the Resource ID.
- **Remove access at the scope where it was created.** An inherited assignment can only be removed at its own level. You cannot delete a subscription-level assignment from inside a child resource group.
- **Changes can take up to 10 minutes to propagate.** If access just changed and still fails, sign out and back in.

## Where it lives

**Portal:** open the scope you want (Management groups / Subscriptions / Resource groups / or a specific resource) > **Access control (IAM)** > **Role assignments** tab > **Add** > **Add role assignment** > pick the role > **Members** (select the principal) > optional conditions / assignment type > **Review + assign**. The scope *is* the blade you opened IAM from — there is no scope dropdown inside the wizard.

**Azure CLI** (no module — the `az` tool): `az role assignment create --assignee "{user-or-objectId}" --role "{roleNameOrId}" --scope "{scopeId}"`. Swap the `--scope` string to change the level.

**Azure PowerShell** (module **Az.Resources**): `New-AzRoleAssignment -SignInName "{user}" -RoleDefinitionName "{role}" -Scope "{scopeId}"`. At resource-group scope you can use `-ResourceGroupName` instead of `-Scope`. Use `-ObjectId` for groups, service principals and managed identities.

## Exam traps

- **"How do I remove an inherited permission at a child scope?"** You don't, not with a role assignment — RBAC is additive, so adding another assignment never subtracts. ==+Assign at a narrower scope==, or remove the assignment at the scope where it was created. A distractor offers "==!add a Reader assignment at the resource== to reduce access"; that is wrong — a second assignment only adds.
- **Role assigned at subscription vs resource group.** Expect a scenario asking the smallest scope that still covers the need. Least privilege means the ==+narrowest scope that works==, not the most convenient.
- **`NotActions` is not a deny.** A tempting wrong answer treats a role's ==!NotActions as blocking an operation globally==. Another assignment can still grant it.
- **Entra roles vs Azure roles.** A ==!Global Administrator does not automatically get access to Azure resources==. ==@Directory== administration and ==@Azure resource== access are separate systems. Watch for this swap.
- **Who can even assign?** Creating an assignment needs ==@Microsoft.Authorization/roleAssignments/write== — held by ==+Owner==, ==+User Access Administrator==, or ==+Role Based Access Control Administrator==. ==!Contributor cannot assign roles== (that authorization action is excluded). Note the split: Owner can do *everything plus* assign; User Access Administrator can *only* manage access, nothing else — a classic distractor pairs them.
- **Propagation delay.** If a question says a just-granted user still can't act, the answer is usually ==+wait / re-authenticate==, not "the assignment failed" — ==!propagation can take up to 10 minutes==.
