## In one sentence
A subscription is the container Azure bills against and scopes access and quota to — you create them, point them at a billing account, move them between directories, and manage who owns and pays for each.

## Why it exists
A subscription is three boundaries wearing one hat: a **billing boundary** (one invoice per subscription), an **access-control boundary** (RBAC and Policy applied at the subscription scope cascade down to every resource group and resource inside it), and a **scale boundary** (quotas and limits are counted per subscription). You already think in scopes and inheritance from RBAC — a subscription is just the widest scope you usually touch, sitting one level below a management group. Separate dev, test and production into separate subscriptions and you get separate bills, separate spending limits and separate access policies for free.

## Mental model
Resources live in resource groups, resource groups live in subscriptions, subscriptions roll up into management groups, and everything folds up to the one Tenant Root Group. Policy and role assignments flow **down** the chain.

## What actually matters
- **A subscription is a billing boundary and an access-control boundary.** Each generates its own invoice, and RBAC/Policy set at the subscription scope inherit to everything inside. This is the single most tested idea.
- **It links to an account (a Microsoft Entra identity) and lives in exactly one directory/tenant and one parent management group.** One account can hold many subscriptions; only one is required.
- **Creating a subscription needs a billing role, not just Azure RBAC.** For a Microsoft Customer Agreement you need Owner or Contributor on the invoice section, billing profile or billing account — or the Azure subscription creator role on the invoice section. MCA, Enterprise Agreement and partner (CSP) subscriptions each have their own path. The old MOSP pay-as-you-go path at signup.azure.com can no longer add subscriptions.
- **Same-directory creation is instant; cross-tenant creation is a request.** Creating a subscription for someone in another tenant raises a *subscription creation request* — the target owner gets an email and must accept within **seven days** or it expires.
- **Transferring a subscription to another directory destroys its RBAC.** All role assignments and custom roles are **permanently deleted** and are not carried over. Managed identities break (system-assigned must be disabled and re-enabled; user-assigned must be recreated). Key Vault keeps its old tenant ID until you update it.
- **Directory transfer and billing-ownership transfer are two different operations.** Moving a subscription to a new tenant does not move who pays; changing billing ownership does not move the directory. You choose each.
- **Quotas are per subscription.** Hitting a quota (e.g. vCPU cores in a region) is a stated reason to create another subscription, or raise a quota-increase support request.

## Where it lives
- **Portal (create / view):** Azure portal > **Subscriptions** > **Add** (Basics > Advanced > Tags > Review + create). List and open each subscription from the same **Subscriptions** blade.
- **Portal (switch context):** **Settings > Directories + subscriptions**, or the filter at the top of the Subscriptions list.
- **Portal (quotas):** Subscription > **Usage + quotas**; request increases with a support request.
- **Portal (RBAC/Policy at scope):** Subscription > **Access control (IAM)** and Subscription > **Policies**.
- **CLI:** `az account list --output table` (list), `az account set --subscription "{name-or-id}"` (switch), `az account show` (current). Programmatic creation uses the Subscription Alias API (`az account alias create`).
- **PowerShell (Az.Accounts):** `Get-AzSubscription` (list), `Set-AzContext -Subscription "{name-or-id}"` (switch). `New-AzSubscriptionAlias` creates one from the alias API.

## Exam traps
- **"Role assignments transfer with the subscription" is ==!false==.** Moving a subscription to another directory ==!deletes all role assignments and custom roles== permanently. You rebuild them in the target. This is the classic distractor.
- **Directory transfer ≠ billing-ownership transfer.** Changing the tenant does not change who is billed, and vice versa. A question that conflates the two is testing exactly this.
- **Resource groups do not nest and cannot be renamed; management groups nest up to six levels.** A subscription has exactly one parent management group. Don't swap these rules between layers.
- **MOSP can no longer add subscriptions at signup.azure.com** — a scenario relying on that path needs MCA or EA instead.
- **A guest (B2B) user cannot be named subscription owner at creation**, and you can't provision subscriptions or resources inside a B2B or B2C tenant.
- **CSP (Cloud Solution Provider) subscriptions cannot change their Microsoft Entra directory** — "transfer the CSP subscription to our tenant" is a trap; use Azure Lighthouse to delegate access instead.
- **Blocking transfers is a subscription policy setting**, not an RBAC deny — know it exists.
